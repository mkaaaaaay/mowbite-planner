# MowBite Planner

A coverage path planner for robot mowers as a ROS 2 node (Humble and Jazzy, Python, shapely). It plans
one mowing area the way a careful person would mow it: first a few loops along the edge and around every
flower bed and tree, then the rest in straight parallel lanes, with turns between them that keep the
wheels rolling instead of spinning on the spot and tearing up the lawn. Out comes a `nav_msgs/Path` in the
`map` frame for the path tracking controller.

It doesn't need MowBite (the web app) or OpenMower to run, but it's made to fit OpenMower: besides its
own service it answers OpenMower's planner service `PlanPath` field by field. For OpenMower as it is
today (ROS 1) there's a ROS 1 node in [`../ros1`](../ros1/README.md) and an [MQTT service](#mqtt-service).

## Packages

| Package | Type | What's in it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `mowbite_planner_interfaces` | ament_cmake | `srv/PlanCoverage.srv`, `srv/PlanPath.srv` (OpenMower's), `msg/Path.msg` |
| `mowbite_planner` | ament_python | the planner (`planner.py`, `maneuvers.py`, `transit.py`, only shapely), OpenMower's paths and map (`export.py`, `mapfile.py`), the node (`node.py`), the MQTT service (`mqtt_service.py`, `docker/`), launch file, parameters, tests, `tools/plot_coverage.py` |

A `.srv` can only be generated by an ament_cmake package, so the interfaces live apart from the Python
node. The planner itself doesn't import anything from ROS: it can be tested and plotted on any PC with
shapely, and wrapped differently if OpenMower's interfaces change.

## The node

```
                       ┌──────────────────────────── mowbite_planner ───────────────────────────┐
 PlanCoverage request  │                                                                        │
 (boundary, obstacles) ├─► ~/plan_coverage ─┐                                                   │
                       │                    │   planner.py (shapely)                            │
 PlanPath request      │                    ├─► 1 perimeter loops  2 remaining area             ├─► ~/coverage_path
 (OpenMower's)         ├─► ~/plan_path ─────┤   3 lanes            4 route: turns + transits    │   nav_msgs/Path, transient local
                       │                    │                                                   │
 std_srvs/Trigger      ├─► ~/plan_from_     │   parameters: mower_width, overlap, turn_radius…  │
 (area in parameters)  │   parameters ──────┘                                                   │
                       └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **`~/plan_coverage`** (`mowbite_planner_interfaces/srv/PlanCoverage`), one area. The request:
  - `boundary`, `obstacles` (parts outside the boundary are ignored) and `drivable_areas` (where the
    mower may drive between parts of the area: other lawns, and navigation areas if the blade is off on
    transits), all `geometry_msgs/Polygon` in the `frame_id` frame.
  - `overrides`: any of the node's parameters by name, for this request only (`rcl_interfaces/Parameter`
    of the parameter's type).
  - on top of those: `mower_width` (≤ 0: the parameter), `overlap` (< 0: the parameter),
    `perimeter_passes` (< 0: the parameter), `use_angle` and `angle` (rad, a fixed lane direction, the
    angle offset and range still apply), `use_start_pose` and `start_pose` (the mower's pose: the path
    starts on the loop next to it, going the way it faces), `use_end_point` and `end_point` (the path
    ends close to it, e.g. the docking station).

  The response: `success`, `message` (a summary or why it failed, with any warnings), `path` (the whole
  route), `section_start` (the first pose of every [section](#sections), mostly just `[0]`), `angle` (the
  lane direction used, 0..π) and `angles` (crosshatch: two), `lane_spacing`, `path_length`, `fingerprint`
  (SHA-256 of the poses) and the segments: `segment_start` and `segment_end` (pose indices),
  `segment_type` (perimeter loop, lane, turn or transit) and `segment_reverse` (the backwards leg of a
  K-turn).
- **`~/plan_path`** (`mowbite_planner_interfaces/srv/PlanPath`): OpenMower's `slic3r_coverage_planner/PlanPath`
  with the same fields and meanings: `fill_type` (`FILL_LINEAR` lanes, `FILL_CONCENTRIC` loops further and
  further in), `angle`, `distance` (the lane spacing), `outer_offset`, `outline_count` (0..255),
  `outline_overlap_count`, `skip_*`, `outline`, `holes`. Everything else comes from the parameters (turn
  radius, K-turns, lane order, waypoint spacing, `max_path_poses`). `mower_width` is the larger of the
  parameter and `distance`. The angle offset and range don't apply, OpenMower adds its own
  `mow_angle_offset`. The answer is `Path[] paths`: the perimeter loops (`is_outline` 1) and the lanes with
  their turns (`is_outline` 0), each a continuous drive, every section its own. A run longer than
  `max_path_poses` (30000, at least 4, OpenMower counts the poses of a path in an int16) is cut into equal
  pieces, each starting on the pose the last one ended on. Every path has at least 3 poses, which
  OpenMower's FTC controller needs. Without a usable plan the answer has no paths, the log says why.
- **`~/plan_from_parameters`** (`std_srvs/srv/Trigger`): plans the area in the parameters `boundary_polygon`
  (`[x0, y0, x1, y1, ...]`) and `obstacles` (`["x0,y0; x1,y1; ...", ...]`), for trying it out.
- **`~/coverage_path`** (`nav_msgs/msg/Path`): every plan, transient local, so RViz or a controller that
  subscribes later still gets the last one (set the Path display's durability to Transient Local).

Every pose has `header.frame_id` = the `frame_id` parameter (`map`) and the time of the plan, and is
`waypoint_spacing` (0.1 m) from the next one. Its orientation is the heading, as a quaternion around z
(`z = sin(yaw / 2)`, `w = cos(yaw / 2)`, worked out directly, no tf). The heading points to the next pose.
On the short backwards leg of a K-turn it points the way the mower faces, against the way it drives,
which is how Nav2's controllers recognise a reversal. Two poses never share a position: OpenMower's FTC
controller would skip one of them.

The same input gives exactly the same poses on the same shapely and GEOS version (OpenMower checks a hash
of them to resume a mow). Across shapely or GEOS versions the last bits of a coordinate or a vertex can
differ, so after such an upgrade OpenMower's checkpoint may not fit any more and an interrupted area
starts again.

## How it plans

1. **Perimeter.** The mowable area is the boundary minus the obstacles, without pieces smaller than
   (mower_width / 2)² (slivers where a recorded outline crosses itself). Its outline shrunk by
   `perimeter_offset` (default half the mower width, so the blade's edge runs on the boundary) is the
   first loop, every further loop is a lane spacing further in (`perimeter_passes`, default 2). The
   obstacles get their loops from the same shrinking, so where a bed is close to the edge the loops go
   around both instead of crossing. Corners of the outline are rounded a little
   (`perimeter_corner_radius`), the inner loops a little less, so no tuft stays between the loops (but see
   [Known limits](#known-limits)). Loops start where the mower already is, the next loop is entered with a
   short slanted hop. A negative `outer_offset` in PlanPath puts the first loop outside the outline and
   lets the mower's centre go as far into the obstacles too, like OpenMower's `outline_offset`.
2. **Remaining area.** What's left after the loops' strips. Where a strip is too narrow for the next loop
   or for lanes but wider than the loops so far reach (0.8 to 1 m with the defaults, less where the loops
   stop earlier), one more loop goes down its middle, before the lanes, unless the lanes reaching into it
   already mow what the loops leave. With `concentric` the same where a part ends between two loops.
3. **Lanes.** Parallel lanes across it, `mower_width * (1 - overlap)` apart, in the direction
   `angle_strategy` picks (default the boundary's longest edge). Where part of the area ends between two
   lanes (an edge running along the lanes, the side of a bed), the neighbouring lane is carried on through
   the headland so no strip stays uncut. `fill_pattern: crosshatch` mows a second set of lanes across the
   first, `concentric` loops further and further in instead of lanes.
4. **Route.** Where an obstacle splits the lanes they're grouped into cells, each mowed as a snake. The
   next cell is the closest one not mowed yet, reached on the shortest way through the free space, along
   the obstacles and the perimeter (a visibility graph searched with A*). With `perimeter_order: last` the
   loops come after the lanes. With an end point (the docking station) the plan ends close to it: the
   lanes in the cell closest to it, the loops (`last`) on the outer loop closest to it, `concentric` on the
   loop closest to it of those nothing else waits for (the innermost going in, the outermost with `last`).
   Without lanes after them the loops end there too, on the innermost loop (the obstacles' loops first)
   or around the obstacle closest to it, whichever is closer; so do the loops down a lawn too narrow for
   lanes.

### Sections

Parts of an area with no way between them (a hedge across the lawn, a passage narrower than the mower, a
corner cut off by a tree close to the fence) are mowed one after the other, each completely, as sections
of their own. `drivable_areas` can join them. In `~/plan_coverage`'s path the next section starts with a
jump, at `section_start`, and the message warns about it: getting there is up to whoever drives the path.
`~/plan_path` gives every section its own paths, and OpenMower drives to the start of every path itself.

### Turns

With a lane spacing of 0.19 m and a turn radius of 0.25 m a half circle doesn't fit between two
neighbouring lanes. The planner tries, gentlest first, and takes the first one that stays inside the
area:

| Turn | Shape | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| U-turn | quarter arc, straight, quarter arc | lanes at least 2 × radius apart |
| bulb | swerve out, loop of more than half a circle, swerve back (forwards only) | about radius × (1 + 2 sin θ) of headland |
| K-turn | quarter arc forwards, a short straight backwards, quarter arc forwards | one radius of headland, `allow_reverse` |
| smaller radius | the same with 0.75, 0.5 × the radius, down to a half circle | |
| detour | quarter arc, shortest way along the edge, quarter arc | lanes 2 radii apart, edge not square to the lanes |
| pivot | turn on the spot by 90°, cross over, turn by 90° | last resort |

`turn_types` sets which of them are allowed and their order. Larger radii always come first, detours and
pivots after all radii.

`lane_order: skip` mows every k-th lane first (and then the ones between), with k = ⌈2 · turn_radius /
lane spacing⌉ (3 with the defaults), so lanes k apart are at least two radii apart: every turn is then a
plain forward U-turn with the full radius. That's the mode for controllers that can't drive backwards. In
a cell of fewer than about 2k lanes the steps are about half the cell wide, too close for that: its turns
are bulbs, also forwards only.

## Build and run

```bash
# in a ROS 2 workspace, these two packages only (ros1/ is a ROS 1 package, colcon can't build it)
cd ~/ros2_ws/src
ln -s /path/to/mowbite/ros2/mowbite_planner_interfaces /path/to/mowbite/ros2/mowbite_planner .
cd ..
rosdep install --from-paths src --ignore-src -y   # python3-shapely among others
colcon build --packages-up-to mowbite_planner
source install/setup.bash

ros2 launch mowbite_planner mowbite_planner.launch.py
```

Plan an area:

```bash
ros2 service call /mowbite_planner/plan_coverage mowbite_planner_interfaces/srv/PlanCoverage "{
  boundary: {points: [{x: 0.0, y: 0.0}, {x: 20.0, y: 0.0}, {x: 20.0, y: 8.0}, {x: 12.0, y: 8.0},
                      {x: 12.0, y: 15.0}, {x: 0.0, y: 15.0}]},
  obstacles: [{points: [{x: 14.0, y: 2.0}, {x: 17.0, y: 2.0}, {x: 17.0, y: 4.0}, {x: 14.0, y: 4.0}]}],
  overrides: [{name: fill_pattern, value: {type: 4, string_value: crosshatch}}]}"
```

(`overrides` is optional, the parameter types are 1 bool, 2 integer, 3 double, 4 string, 9 string array.)
Or put an area into `config/mowbite_planner.yaml` (`boundary_polygon`, `obstacles`) and call
`ros2 service call /mowbite_planner/plan_from_parameters std_srvs/srv/Trigger`.

Parameters (all in `config/mowbite_planner.yaml`, doubles need a decimal point there):

| Parameter | Default | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `frame_id` | `map` | frame of the polygons and the path |
| `mower_width` | 0.22 | cutting width [m], 0.01..5 |
| `overlap` | 0.15 | 0.15 = neighbouring lanes overlap by 15 % of the width, 0..0.95 |
| `perimeter_passes` | 2 | loops along the edge and around every obstacle, 0..255 |
| `perimeter_offset` | -1.0 | first loop's distance from the boundary [m], negative = mower_width / 2 |
| `perimeter_order` | `first` | loops `first` or `last` (last mows over the marks the turns leave) |
| `perimeter_direction` | `auto` | `ccw`, `cw` or `auto` (the way the mower faces) |
| `lane_overlap_passes` | 0 | lanes reach this many loops further out (OpenMower's `outline_overlap_count`), 0..255 |
| `fill_pattern` | `lanes` | `lanes`, `crosshatch` or `concentric` |
| `crosshatch_angle_deg` | 90.0 | direction of crosshatch's second lanes to the first |
| `angle_strategy` | `longest_edge` | `longest_edge`, `min_width` (across the narrowest width), `optimal` (fewest lanes and cells, tried `angle_step_deg` apart) or `fixed` (`fixed_angle_deg`) |
| `fixed_angle_deg` | 0.0 | lane direction for `fixed`, 0 = +x, counter-clockwise |
| `angle_offset_deg` | 0.0 | added to the lane direction, e.g. different stripes every mow |
| `angle_min_deg`, `angle_max_deg` | `.nan` | the lane direction stays in this range, both or neither |
| `angle_step_deg` | 5.0 | between the directions `optimal` tries, 0.5..90 |
| `turn_radius` | 0.25 | radius of the turns [m], 0..5 |
| `allow_reverse` | true | K-turns |
| `turn_types` | all | `[u_turn, bulb, k_turn, detour, pivot]`, the ones allowed, preferred first |
| `lane_order` | `snake` | `snake` or `skip` |
| `route_order` | `nearest` | cells `nearest` first, or `optimized`: the order with the shortest drives between them |
| `perimeter_corner_radius` | 0.15 | rounding of the loops' outline corners [m], 0 = sharp |
| `waypoint_spacing` | 0.10 | distance between poses [m], 0 = only where the direction changes |
| `transit_clearance` | 0.05 | how far transits stay from the outer loop and obstacle loops [m] |
| `simplify_tolerance` | 0.01 | the boundary and obstacles are simplified this much first [m] |
| `min_lane_length` | 0.10 | shorter lane pieces are left out [m] |
| `nested_areas` | true | a mowing area lying in the one planned (90 % of it at least, an area of its own with another angle or pattern) is left out and mowed round, it gets its own plan; for the map's areas (MQTT service, ROS 1 node) |
| `narrow_parts` | `lanes` | where the lanes would lie in a part narrower than a U-turn (2 turn radii: a path, a strip between beds): `lanes` anyway, or `loops` further in there, the pieces they come apart into driven on the way round. Areas without such parts keep their plan |
| `robot_width` | -1.0 | width of the mower's body [m], negative = not set |
| `robot_front` | -1.0 | how far the body reaches ahead of the mower's centre (base_link) [m], negative = not set |
| `robot_rear` | -1.0 | how far it reaches behind the centre [m], negative = not set. With all three the turns, lane ends, loops and drives keep the body in (see `edges`): lane ends move back until the turn's arcs fit, loop corners get larger arcs; `report` lists the poses where it still sticks out (`body_out`) |
| `edges` | `recorded` | `recorded`: the outline and obstacles are where the mower's centre drove, the body may reach half its width past them. `hard`: walls, nothing of the body past them, the centre keeps `robot_width` / 2 off them |
| `blade_offset` | 0.0 | the blade's centre this far left of the mower's [m] (negative: right), the loops go round with the blade on their outer side, -1..1 |
| `blade_ahead` | 0.0 | the blade's centre this far ahead of the mower's centre (OpenMower's base_link between the drive wheels) [m], negative = behind; the lanes start and end that much earlier, -1..1 |
| `bend_max_gap` | 0.0 | a row split by an obstacle with at most this much between its pieces becomes one lane bending around it [m], 0 = split |
| `bend_max_offset` | 0.5 | how far such a bend may leave its lane's line [m] |
| `transit_edge_distance` | 0.0 | transits keep this far off the edges where that costs little more [m], 0 = the shortest way |
| `transit_edge_cost` | 2.0 | every metre of a transit closer to the edge than that counts this much more |
| `publish_path` | true | publish every plan on `~/coverage_path` |
| `max_path_poses` | 30000 | `~/plan_path` cuts longer paths, at least 4 |

```bash
cd ros2/mowbite_planner

```

`tools/plot_coverage.py` draws the boundary, the obstacles, the loops, lanes, turns (backing up dashed)
and transits, with arrows for the headings, and prints lengths, turn counts and the planning time.

The planner's tests run against shapely 1.7 (Python 3.8, as on ROS Noetic), 1.8 (Ubuntu 22.04,
Raspberry Pi OS bookworm) and 2.x. Planning on a PC with shapely 2.1: a garden of 300 m² with two
obstacles 0.06 s, a 33 × 21 m orchard with 40 trees 1 s, a lawn of 1700 m² with a recorded outline (a
point every 10 cm, 3 cm noise) and two obstacles 0.5 s. `angle_strategy: optimal` counts the lanes on
the outline as it is, read once for all angles: on 2500 m² recorded every 2 cm with seven obstacles it
takes 2.7 s instead of 1.5 s. A Raspberry Pi CM4 needs roughly four to six times as long. The node needs
about 20 µs a pose on top for the messages (the 300 m² garden has 18000 poses).

## With OpenMower

OpenMower runs on ROS 1 Noetic, also on OpenMower OS, and plans every area with its slic3r planner through
`slic3r_coverage_planner/plan_path`. Two ways to use the MowBite Planner with it today:

- **In place of slic3r**: the ROS 1 node in [`../ros1`](../ros1/README.md) answers that service, so
  `mower_logic` mows the MowBite Planner's plans. It goes into your own build of OpenMower's image
  ([how](../ros1/README.md#your-own-openmower-image)).
- **Next to it, over MQTT** ([below](#mqtt-service)): for OpenMower's Planner Studio and for previews of
  the mower's map, without changing OpenMower. `mower_logic` keeps mowing slic3r's plans then.

A ROS 2 port of OpenMower can call `~/plan_path`: start the node with `node_name:=slic3r_coverage_planner`,
then the service is `/slic3r_coverage_planner/plan_path`, with the same fields. ROS 2 matches services by
their full type name though: the port has to use `mowbite_planner_interfaces/srv/PlanPath` (and
`mowbite_planner_interfaces/msg/Path`), with `slic3r_coverage_planner`'s types unchanged it can't call it.
What the request doesn't carry comes from the parameters: set `mower_width` to the real blade width
(OpenMower's `tool_width` is the lane spacing, `distance` in the request), and for OpenMower's FTC
controller, which only drives forwards, `allow_reverse: false` and `lane_order: skip`.

`~/plan_coverage` gives more than `PlanPath` can carry. If OpenMower's side can be changed, these make the
most of it:

- **Drive the route as one path.** Every separate path starts with driving to its first pose and turning
  on the spot there. The route from `~/plan_coverage` is continuous within a section, transits included.
- **A controller that can back up**, e.g. Nav2's regulated pure pursuit with `allow_reversing`. Then K-turns
  keep the full turn radius in a narrow headland. FTC with `forward_only: false` reverses but doesn't
  handle the change of direction.
- **Use the segments.** `segment_type` says where turns and transits are (slower in turns, blade off on
  long transits), `segment_reverse` where the mower backs up.
- **Pass the mower's pose** (`use_start_pose`), so the plan starts on the loop next to the mower, and the
  docking station as `end_point`.
- **Leave the angle to the planner** (`use_angle: false`): along the longest edge gives the fewest turns.
  OpenMower's own automatic angle is the direction of the first two metres of the outline.

## MQTT service

`mowbite_planner/mqtt_service.py` answers JSON-RPC 2.0 requests over MQTT like OpenMower's own RPC:
requests on `<prefix>rpc/request`, answers on `<prefix>rpc/response`. OpenMower's `xbot_monitoring` leaves
the methods starting with `ext.` to other services, these are this one's. Like OpenMower's own RPC they
need `"jsonrpc": "2.0"` and a string `id`; `xbot_monitoring` answers anything else with an error, this
service then stays quiet.

| Method | Params | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ext.planner.plan` | Planner Studio's request (OpenMower's app, xtech/openmower-app): `area`, `obstacles`, `nav_areas`, `params` (its names: `tool_width`, `headland_rounds`, `min_turning_radius`, `ring_order`, `disk_lateral_offset`, `transit_wall_fade`, `transit_wall_risk`, `merge_max_gap`, `bend_max_dev`, `order` greedy/ortools, or the planner's own) | Planner Studio's stages, lineage, stats and report (parameters the planner doesn't know are listed as not used), the paths under `mowbite` |
| `ext.mowbite_planner.plan` | `area_id` (id, name or index of a mowing area of the mower's map) or `outline`, `obstacles`, `nav_areas`; optional `angle`, `outline_count`, `outline_overlap_count`, `outline_offset`, `angle_min`, `angle_max` in place of the area's properties, `settings` (any planner setting), `start`, `end`, `poses`, `report`, `trace` | like OpenMower's `mowing.plan`: `paths` with `is_outline` and `points` (simplified to 1 cm, in mm), plus their segments, `fingerprint`, `warnings`, `stats`, with `poses: true` every pose, with `report: true` what's wrong with the plan (`hard`), what's worth knowing (`soft`) and how much is mowed once, more than once (`double_mowed_pct`) and not at all, with `trace: true` where everything comes from (`stages`, `lineage`: the area, loop levels, cells, loops, lanes, every segment and what it was made from) |
| `ext.mowbite_planner.info` | none | version, settings, whether the map and parameters are in |

Points are `[x, y]` or `{"x": x, "y": y}`, `start` may have a heading. The service keeps the map and the
parameters the mower publishes (`map/json`, `params/json`, retained). `ext.mowbite_planner.plan` plans
with the settings the ROS 1 node gets for the area: `PLANNER_SETTINGS`, the mower's parameters
(`tool_width` is the lane spacing, `outline_count`, `outline_offset`, ...), the area's properties (the
request's in their place, like `mowing.plan`; an `outline` is an area of its own) and its `planner`
object, the request's `settings`. The lanes go the way `mower_logic` asks for: the area's `angle` or
OpenMower's own (towards the first point more than 2 m from the first) plus `mow_angle_offset` (brought
into -180..180 like OpenMower does), with `mow_angle_offset_is_absolute` the offset alone. An `angle` or
`angle_strategy` in the `planner` object or `settings` beats that, one in `PLANNER_SETTINGS` only for
areas without an `angle` of their own. Not in the preview: the `mow_angle_increment` OpenMower adds after
every finished mow, that's the mower's own state. With `area_id` the rest comes from the map like on the
mower: the area's obstacles and the areas to mow around in it, the other lawns for ways between its parts
(not the navigation areas or areas set to not mowable, the mower mows a path from start to end) and the
docking station (start and end). The outline and the obstacles are planned in float32, as `mower_logic`
sends them. A bad request is answered with error -32602 and the reason. Requests are planned one after
another.

```bash
mosquitto_pub -h <mower> -t rpc/request \
  -m '{"jsonrpc": "2.0", "id": "1", "method": "ext.mowbite_planner.plan", "params": {"area_id": "Front lawn"}}'
```

On the mower it runs as a container: `docker compose up -d` in `ros2/mowbite_planner/docker` builds and
starts it (`docker/Dockerfile`: Python on alpine with shapely and paho-mqtt, amd64 and arm64, about 39 MB
to download and 145 MB unpacked). It uses host networking, the mower's broker is on 127.0.0.1:1883. The
environment:

- `MOWER_MQTT_HOST` (127.0.0.1), `MOWER_MQTT_PORT` (1883), `MOWER_MQTT_USER`, `MOWER_MQTT_PASSWORD`:
  the broker.
- `MOWER_MQTT_PREFIX`: topic prefix, only if OpenMower uses one.
- `PLANNER_SETTINGS`: a JSON object of planner settings (the names in `PlannerConfig`, angles in rad).
  `compose.yaml` sets `{"allow_reverse": false, "lane_order": "skip"}` for OpenMower's FTC controller.
  Add `mower_width` with the blade's width. With the ROS 1 node on the mower, its `settings` and
  `mower_width` here make the previews its plans.

Without Docker it needs shapely and paho-mqtt (1.6 or 2.x): `python3 -m mowbite_planner.mqtt_service` in
`ros2/mowbite_planner`, or `mowbite_planner_mqtt` from the colcon build.

## Known limits

- Very pointed corners keep a small tuft: no loop reaches the tip.
- Where a corner of the outline is cut off (a chamfer) or recorded as a few short edges, the outer loop
  rounds it less than the inner loops count on: a tuft of a few cm² can stay between them.
- With `perimeter_passes: 0` a strip can stay along edges parallel to the lanes.
- Perimeter loops follow the GPS jitter of a recorded outline vertex by vertex. Reduce its points first
  (MowBite's map editor does that) or raise `simplify_tolerance`.
- `geometry_msgs/Polygon` is float32: steps of 1 mm or less up to 8 km from the map origin. Further out
  the node warns, use a local map frame.
- Points have to be numbers within 10⁷ m of the map origin and `turn_radius` at most 10 m (the node: 5 m),
  anything else is refused with the reason.
